RESEARCH: WHAT EXISTS TODAY
1. Pro-rata streaming (Spotify model): all revenue pooled, split by share of total streams. Spotify pays ~$0.003-0.005/stream (~315,000 streams to gross $1,000). Tidal ~$0.0128, Apple ~$0.01, YouTube Music ~$0.0071, Amazon ~$0.004. Core criticism: pool math favors mega-artists and incentivizes stream-count inflation.
2. User-centric / fan-powered (SoundCloud FPR): each listener's subscription is split only among artists THEY played. Case studies showed +217% for mid-tier artists. Majors refuse to adopt it: requires renegotiating every rights-holder license globally, and a French study found only marginal gains for small artists. Key lesson: user-centric CANNOT win inside DSPs - the relicensing wall is structural. It can only win in a layer the artist controls.
3. Artist-centric (Deezer x UMG): pro-rata plus boosts - 2x for artists with 1,000+ monthly listens from 500+ unique listeners, 2x again for actively searched artists, non-artist noise demonetized, per-user monthly stream count capped at 1,000 (fractional beyond). A patch on pro-rata, not a new mechanism.
4. Direct-to-fan (Bandcamp/Patreon): artist keeps ~85-92%. Proven, but it is storefront-shaped: passive, human-buyer-only, no licensing automation.
5. AI training licensing (the new money): Udio has struck opt-in licensing deals with UMG, WMG, and Merlin (indie labels); ElevenLabs did an opt-in deal with Merlin + Kobalt (Sept 2025); WMG settled with Suno via license. Compensation structures are UNDISCLOSED and flow through labels. Independent artists currently have NO direct rail to price and sell AI rights. Udio itself is pivoting to a walled-garden licensed-remix platform in 2026.

THE GAP THE RESEARCH EXPOSES
Every existing mechanism assumes the BUYER of music rights is a human (a listener, a music supervisor, a label). In the AI age the fastest-growing buyers are MACHINES and the companies behind them: model trainers, AI video/game/podcast generators, agentic apps that need soundtrack-on-demand. There is no mechanism that lets an independent artist expose priced, scoped, instantly-purchasable rights to that demand. That is the invention space.

MY PROPOSED MECHANISM (for Codex to challenge): "OPEN LICENSE MANIFEST" - three parts.
P1. Machine-readable rights layer: every track gets a public manifest (JSON) declaring scoped licenses and prices - podcast sync, game loop, brand sync, remix/stem use, AI-training opt-in, AI-generation opt-in - each with instant checkout. Think "robots.txt + price list for music rights." A human OR an AI agent can discover, price, and buy a license in seconds with zero negotiation.
P2. User-centric fan memberships OUTSIDE the DSPs: fans subscribe to artists/collectives directly; 100% of a fan's payment routes to artists they chose (minus flat fee). This deploys the SoundCloud lesson where no relicensing wall exists.
P3. Transparent split settlement: every dollar from P1/P2 is split at the moment of sale per the on-record collaborator ledger; payout instructions are exportable and auditable by every collaborator.
ECONOMICS: flat 5-8% platform fee on transactions only. No pool, no black box. Creator keeps 92-95%, inverting the DSP ratio where the platform side controls ~70% of the value chain.
WHY INNOVATIVE: nobody serves machine buyers today; licensing rails for AI are locked inside major-label deals with undisclosed terms. This gives independents the same rail, self-serve.
WHY REALISTIC: P1+P3 are static-site + serverless + Stripe territory - deployable on what we already built. P2 is Stripe subscriptions. No token/blockchain required.
